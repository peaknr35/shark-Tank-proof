# The founder interview

Ask one question at a time and wait for the answer. Use their words. If they
give you files, read those first and skip what the files already answer.

## About them (fills founder/me.md)

1. In one sentence, what do you do, and for whom?
2. What did you do before this, and what are you known for?
3. Which country, and what are you: an individual, a small business, inside a
   company, a university, a nonprofit?
4. Where can people see your work (a site, a profile, a repo)?

## About what is real today (fills the ledger)

5. What exists right now that someone could see or run? Has anyone outside your
   team used it?
6. Have you spoken to anyone who has this problem? Who, when, and what did they
   say about what it costs them today?
7. Has anyone paid, signed, pre-ordered or booked anything? Show me.
8. Which numbers have you actually measured (a test run, a timing, a quote) and
   which are estimates?
9. Where has it broken, and what did you change?
10. What is still unproven? What would hurt most if it turned out wrong?

## About the next yes (fills opportunities/ and the gaps)

11. Who has to say yes for your next step: a customer, a funder, someone inside
    your business, or the people who will use it?
12. What do you want from them: a call, a pilot, money, a budget, adoption?
13. How much money or time would it take, and what would it pay for?
14. Who could vouch for you, partner with you, or test it for you?
15. What must stay private?

When it is done, file the originals, log the claims, then read back the three
strongest real claims and the three biggest gaps, and ask whether they are right.
