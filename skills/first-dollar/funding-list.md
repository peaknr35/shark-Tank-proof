# Funding list: places to look

A starting list of grants, procurement portals and programs that pay for a
pilot, an audit or a question before the product is finished. Pick two or
three that fit the founder's country, field and organisation type.

**Every amount, deadline and rule below was true on the date it was checked.**
They change. Open the official page and confirm before anything goes into
`founder/opportunities/`; if you cannot confirm it, write "check".

## US federal R&D money for small businesses
Equity-free federal R&D money for US small businesses, plus the two free registrations you need before you can apply.

### [NSF America's Seed Fund (SBIR/STTR): Project Pitch](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/project-pitch/)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Phase I up to $305,000; Phase II up to $1,250,000; no equity taken
- **Who it's for:** US small businesses with fewer than 500 employees, at least 50% owned by US citizens or permanent residents, doing the funded work in the US. The lead researcher must work for the company at least 20 hours a week; no advanced degree is needed.
- **How you start:** Submit a Project Pitch in four short sections: the technology innovation, the technical objectives and challenges, the market opportunity, and the company and team. NSF replies in about 1 to 2 months, and only invited companies can send a full Phase I proposal.
- **Why it's here:** It is the friendliest front door to federal R&D money, because a short pitch gets you a yes or no before you write a full proposal.
- **Caution:** Check first. NSF funds deep technology with real technical risk. A workflow build that only combines existing AI tools is unlikely to be invited. Limit of two pitches per company in any 12 months. Next full proposal deadlines: 4 Nov 2026, 4 Mar 2027, 7 Jul 2027. SBIR and STTR lapsed from 30 Sep 2025 until they were reauthorized on 13 Apr 2026 (now through 30 Sep 2031).
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [SBIR.gov funding opportunities (all agencies)](https://www.sbir.gov/topics)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Varies by agency; SBIR.gov guide: Phase I $50,000 to $275,000, Phase II $750,000 to $1.8 million
- **Who it's for:** US small businesses with fewer than 500 employees that want equity-free R&D money from any federal agency that runs SBIR or STTR (for example Defense, Energy, NASA, Health and Human Services, NSF and Education).
- **How you start:** Search the funding opportunities list by keyword (try artificial intelligence, education, workforce, automation), filter to Open, then follow each topic to the agency's own solicitation page before you write anything.
- **Why it's here:** One search box shows topics from every SBIR agency with open and close dates, so you can see in ten minutes whether any agency is asking for what you build.
- **Caution:** Check first. SBIR.gov warns that its topic copies may be out of date, so always check the agency's own page. Each agency sets its own amounts. The 2026 law added limits on how many proposals one company can submit.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [ED/IES SBIR (education technology)](https://ies.ed.gov/funding/research/programs/small-business-innovation-research-sbir)
*Helps you get: a funder, people who use it*
- **What you can get:** Phase I $250,000 for 9 months; Phase II $1,000,000 for 2 years
- **Who it's for:** For-profit US small businesses and their partners building education technology products. Good for educators and edtech builders.
- **How you start:** Before the yearly solicitation is released, email the program staff listed on the page to talk through your idea. Once a solicitation opens, staff can no longer give advice, and proposals are due about 60 days later.
- **Why it's here:** It is the main federal R&D fund aimed squarely at edtech, and the gap between solicitations is the one time staff will give feedback on your idea.
- **Caution:** Check first. The FY2026 round appears to have closed in June 2026 (date from third-party listings, not confirmed on the IES page). No FY2027 date was shown on the page, so get ready now and watch for the release.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [NIH SBIR/STTR (America's Seed Fund powered by NIH)](https://seed.nih.gov/)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Guideline budgets: Phase I $335,937; Phase II $2,239,578
- **Who it's for:** US small businesses building health and biomedical products and services. STTR requires a partner nonprofit research institution such as a university; SBIR does not.
- **How you start:** Read NIH's SBIR and STTR basics, check eligibility and current funding opportunities, and start the several registrations NIH says you need before you can apply.
- **Why it's here:** NIH says $1.4 billion is available to small businesses through the program, the largest SBIR pot for anyone working in health.
- **Caution:** Check first. Budget figures are guidelines, and individual NIH institutes can set their own limits. Start registrations early because you cannot submit without them.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [NSF I-Corps](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/initiatives/i-corps)
*Helps you get: a customer, a funder*
- **What you can get:** National Teams: up to $50,000 (includes a $10,000 participation fee paid from the award)
- **Who it's for:** Teams of 3 to 5 linked to a US college or university: a technical lead, an entrepreneurial lead and an industry mentor. Researchers without a recent NSF award first complete a regional I-Corps program through a Hub.
- **How you start:** Attend one of NSF's monthly introductory webinars, then contact the I-Corps Hub nearest you about its regional training.
- **Why it's here:** It is a seven-week customer discovery program, which builds exactly the evidence a first customer or funder wants to see.
- **Caution:** Check first. Built for university research. A learner without a campus link would join a university team as the industry mentor or entrepreneurial lead, or ask a Hub whether its regional program takes outside teams.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [SAM.gov entity registration and Unique Entity ID](https://sam.gov/entity-registration)
*Helps you get: a funder, a customer*
- **What you can get:** Free; renew every 365 days
- **Who it's for:** Any US business or organization that wants to apply for federal grants or bid on federal contracts.
- **How you start:** Create a Login.gov account, then choose a full registration (needed to apply for awards or bid as the prime) or a Unique Entity ID only (enough for some subaward reporting). Have your legal business name and physical address ready.
- **Why it's here:** No federal grant or contract can be awarded to you without it, so do it before you need it, not the week of a deadline.
- **Caution:** Check first. Never pay a third party to register. GSA says registration is always free and warns about scammers asking for fees. Free help is available from APEX Accelerators (see Help and search tools).
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [Grants.gov](https://www.grants.gov/applicants/applicant-registration)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Free to register; grant amounts vary
- **Who it's for:** Organizations, and some individuals, applying for US federal grants from any agency.
- **How you start:** Browse Search Grants first. To apply, register your organization in SAM.gov, then create a Grants.gov account with the same email as your SAM.gov E-Business point of contact and add an organization profile using your Unique Entity ID.
- **Why it's here:** It is the single federal site where grant opportunities from every agency are posted, so one saved search covers them all.
- **Caution:** Check first. Most federal grants are for organizations, not individuals. Next scheduled system maintenance: 21 to 23 Nov 2026.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

## Government contracts and RFPs
Public buyers post what they want to buy, so register as a vendor, set alerts for your service codes and answer small requests first.

### [State procurement portals (example: MyFloridaMarketPlace)](https://www.dms.myflorida.com/business_operations/state_purchasing/myfloridamarketplace/mfmp_vendors)
*Helps you get: a customer*
- **What you can get:** Varies by contract; Florida vendor registration is free
- **Who it's for:** Any business that wants to sell to a US state government. Every state runs its own portal; Florida's is MyFloridaMarketPlace.
- **How you start:** Search '[your state] procurement vendor registration', register as a vendor on that state's portal, and pick the commodity codes that match your services (consulting, IT services, training) so bid alerts reach you.
- **Why it's here:** In Florida, as in many states, you must be a registered vendor before you can respond to a solicitation, so registering early keeps doors open.
- **Caution:** Check first. Florida charges vendors a transaction fee on payments received (1% in a recent state term contract; older DMS documents show 0.7%), so confirm the current rate with DMS. Each state's rules and codes differ.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [Texas ESBD (Electronic State Business Daily)](https://www.txsmartbuy.gov/esbd)
*Helps you get: a customer*
- **What you can get:** Varies by solicitation
- **Who it's for:** Businesses selling to Texas state agencies, cities, counties, school districts, community colleges and universities.
- **How you start:** Search open solicitations by keyword or NIGP commodity code; no sign-in is needed to look. Read each solicitation and its attachments for how to respond.
- **Why it's here:** A clear example of a state bid board: one search covers hundreds of Texas public buyers, including many school districts and universities.
- **Caution:** Check first. Looking is open to all, but each buyer sets its own response rules, and some may require vendor registration. The same site hosts the Centralized Master Bidders List.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [BidNet Direct](https://www.bidnetdirect.com/)
*Helps you get: a customer*
- **What you can get:** Free registration; paid plans add wider alerts
- **Who it's for:** Vendors selling to state and local governments, school districts and other US public agencies, organized into state purchasing groups.
- **How you start:** Register as a vendor using the free option, join your state's purchasing group, and set your categories so matching bids reach your inbox.
- **Why it's here:** Many towns, counties and school districts publish and distribute their bids through it, and the free tier covers the agencies in your group.
- **Caution:** Check first. BidNet says there is no charge to view, download or respond to bids from participating agencies, so try the free tier first. Paid plan prices on older BidNet pages may be out of date.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [DemandStar](https://network.demandstar.com/)
*Helps you get: a customer*
- **What you can get:** Free account; paid subscriptions available
- **Who it's for:** Businesses selling to US local governments: cities, counties, school districts, housing authorities, airports and similar buyers.
- **How you start:** Register as a business for the free account and list the goods and services you offer so you are notified of matching bids.
- **Why it's here:** Over 1,400 agencies post directly on it, and it is free for governments, so many small public buyers use it.
- **Caution:** Check first. What the free account covers versus the paid plans is shown only on the business pricing page; check it before upgrading.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [SAM.gov Contract Opportunities](https://sam.gov/opportunities)
*Helps you get: a customer*
- **What you can get:** Varies by notice
- **Who it's for:** US businesses that want to sell to federal agencies.
- **How you start:** Search without an account. Create a free account to save searches, follow notices and join interested vendor lists. To bid you need a full SAM.gov registration.
- **Why it's here:** Federal buyers post pre-solicitation notices here as well as RFPs, so you can see work coming and introduce yourself before the request is written.
- **Caution:** Check first. Federal contracting has its own rules and paperwork. Get free help from an APEX Accelerator before your first bid.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [School district procurement pages (example: LAUSD)](https://procurement.lausd.org/)
*Helps you get: a customer, people who use it*
- **What you can get:** Varies
- **Who it's for:** Businesses selling services, training or software to US K-12 school districts.
- **How you start:** Search '[district name] procurement' or 'purchasing', find the vendor registration and solicitations pages, and check whether the district also posts on BidNet Direct or DemandStar.
- **Why it's here:** Districts post solicitations and run vendor sessions, like LAUSD's vendor roundtables, which are a direct way to meet the people who buy.
- **Caution:** Check first. Large districts have formal compliance and prequalification steps. A pilot with one school may go through a smaller purchasing route, so ask the purchasing office which applies.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [College and university procurement pages (example: University of Florida)](https://procurement.ufl.edu/)
*Helps you get: a customer, your own business*
- **What you can get:** Varies
- **Who it's for:** Businesses selling consulting, training or software to US colleges and universities.
- **How you start:** Search '[college name] procurement' and look for the Doing Business, Schedule of Bids and Supplier Portal pages; register as a supplier so a department can buy from you.
- **Why it's here:** Universities post their bids publicly, and a staff member who wants your work will need you set up as a supplier before they can pay you.
- **Caution:** Check first. UF warns of fake purchase orders and quote requests posing as the university. Confirm any unexpected request directly with the procurement office.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

## UK, EU and other countries
National innovation agencies and grant finders outside the US; several changed in 2025 and 2026, so check status before you plan around them.

### [UKRI Funding Finder (including Innovate UK)](https://www.ukri.org/opportunity/)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Varies by call
- **Who it's for:** UK businesses, researchers and organisations. Eligibility is set per call; many Innovate UK calls need a UK registered business.
- **How you start:** Filter the finder by Innovate UK and Open or Upcoming, read the call, then apply through the Innovation Funding Service. Open now: Efficient, Structured and Controllable AI Systems (single UK SMEs, up to £1.66 million listed, closes 18 Nov 2026) and three BridgeAI Deployment Catalyst competitions (up to £5 million, close 8 Dec 2026).
- **Why it's here:** It is one list of every UKRI council's calls, and Innovate UK has live AI competitions right now.
- **Caution:** Check first. Innovate UK Smart grants, the old any-topic route, are 'closed for new applications' according to UKRI (trade sources say paused since early 2025), and UKRI names no direct replacement. BridgeAI calls are collaborative, need a UK AI SME lead and a medium or large deployer.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [ARIA (Advanced Research + Invention Agency)](https://www.aria.org.uk/funding-opportunities)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Varies by call
- **Who it's for:** Researchers, companies and teams working on ambitious programmes in ARIA's opportunity spaces. Eligibility is set per call.
- **How you start:** Open a programme's call page and submit a concept paper or proposal in the format it asks for. Open now: Safeguarded AI: Cybersecurity (£20 million) and Scaling Trust Tracks 2 and 3 (£49.8 million), both rolling with the current batch closing 31 Oct 2026.
- **Why it's here:** Its Safeguarded AI and Scaling Trust programmes fund work on making AI systems trustworthy, which can overlap with assurance-focused builds.
- **Caution:** Check first. ARIA funds high-risk research programmes, not small business tools; most learners would join as a partner on a research team. Who can apply, including non-UK applicants, is stated per call, not on the home page.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [BRAID (Bridging Responsible AI Divides, AHRC)](https://braiduk.org/open-funding-calls)
*Helps you get: a funder, people who use it*
- **What you can get:** 2026 Fellowships: about £620,000 in total for around five projects
- **Who it's for:** UK arts and humanities researchers paired with non-academic partners (businesses, public bodies, charities, community groups) that have a stake in UK AI. Creative Commissions are for UK-based creatives.
- **How you start:** If you run a business or charity, become the non-academic partner: browse BRAID's partner page or approach a UK arts and humanities researcher, help shape the research challenge, and sign the partner letter of support.
- **Why it's here:** One of the few responsible AI funds that pays for work inside real organisations, and businesses can take part as partners.
- **Caution:** Check first. Deadlines are close: Fellowships close 5 Nov 2026 at 17:00 GMT; Creative Commissions 2 Nov 2026; the Edinburgh Festivals Accessibility Challenge 27 Oct 2026. The lead applicant must be employed by an eligible UK research organisation. Projects run up to 12 months plus 3 months of impact work.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [Find a Grant (GOV.UK)](https://www.find-government-grants.service.gov.uk/)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Varies; filter by bands from under £10,000 to over £5 million
- **Who it's for:** UK individuals, private sector businesses, non-profits and public bodies looking for UK government grants.
- **How you start:** Filter by who can apply (Private sector or Non-profit) and your nation or region, then sign up to be emailed when new grants are added.
- **Why it's here:** It is the official UK government grant search with email alerts, so you do not have to check each department.
- **Caution:** Check first. Still a beta service, and many listings are sector schemes (energy, farming, transport) rather than R&D, so expect few direct AI matches.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [EIC Accelerator (European Innovation Council)](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/eic-funding-opportunities/eic-accelerator_en)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Grant below €2.5 million; investment of €1 million to €10 million
- **Who it's for:** Startups and SMEs, including people planning to set one up, in EU member states (for example Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands, Ireland) and Horizon Europe associated countries. UK applicants can apply for the grant-only option.
- **How you start:** Submit a Step 1 short proposal at any time: a short form, a pitch deck of up to 10 slides and a video pitch of up to 3 minutes. Proposals are batched on the first Tuesday of each month, with feedback in about 4 to 6 weeks.
- **Why it's here:** Step 1 is light enough to test whether a European funder sees your idea as fundable before you write a full proposal.
- **Caution:** Check first. Built for breakthrough technology close to market (TRL 6 to 8) and very competitive; not a fit for services or consulting. The page lists 2026 full-proposal cut-offs only, the last being 4 Nov 2026.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [Enterprise Ireland Innovation Voucher](https://www.enterprise-ireland.com/en/supports/innovation-voucher)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Standard €10,000; co-funded up to €20,000; valid 18 months
- **Who it's for:** Small and medium-sized limited companies registered in Ireland.
- **How you start:** Pick a question you need outside expertise on, choose a registered knowledge provider such as a higher education institute, and apply online through Enterprise Ireland. Vouchers are available all year.
- **Why it's here:** A low-barrier way for an Irish small business to pay for expert help on an AI or process question, without waiting for a call.
- **Caution:** Check first. Older Enterprise Ireland FAQs still show €5,000 vouchers; go by the current page. The voucher pays the knowledge provider, not your own time. The FAQ says sole traders are not eligible.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [NRC IRAP (Canada)](https://nrc.canada.ca/en/support-technology-innovation)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Varies: advisory services, financial support for technology innovation, funding toward hiring young graduates
- **Who it's for:** Canadian small and medium-sized businesses building their innovation capacity and bringing ideas to market, including AI innovation.
- **How you start:** Call 1-877-994-4727 toll-free or contact an IRAP representative to be connected with an Industrial Technology Advisor and talk through your project.
- **Why it's here:** The advisor relationship comes first and money second, which suits early work that is not ready for a formal grant application.
- **Caution:** Check first. The government plans full implementation of a new Canada Innovation Corporation by 2026-27; until then IRAP stays with NRC, but contact routes may change. Funding is decided case by case.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [business.gov.au Grants and programs finder (Australia)](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Varies by program
- **Who it's for:** Businesses operating in Australia, including sole traders, partnerships, companies, trusts and not-for-profits.
- **How you start:** Select Start now and answer a few questions (under 5 minutes), filter by objective (there is an AI option), state and grant status, then star a shortlist and email it to yourself.
- **Why it's here:** It pulls programs from across government into one guided search with an AI filter, which saves hours of checking separate sites.
- **Caution:** Check first. A shortlist is only a starting point; each program's own page sets eligibility and dates.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [MBIE Innovation Services funding (New Zealand)](https://www.business.govt.nz/tax-and-money/innovation-funding)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** New to R&D Grant: 40% of eligible costs, up to NZ$400,000
- **Who it's for:** New Zealand businesses doing R&D. The New to R&D Grant is for NZ-incorporated companies, limited partnerships, or Māori incorporations or trusts that have spent under NZ$150,000 on R&D in the past 3 years.
- **How you start:** Contact Innovation Services before applying; their Navigators confirm eligibility, then you apply through the Innovation Services online grants portal.
- **Why it's here:** It is now the main door for NZ business R&D grants, and the New to R&D Grant is open for applications.
- **Caution:** Check first. Callaghan Innovation is being disestablished and its grants have moved to MBIE's Innovation Services (secondary sources date the transfer to 1 Dec 2025), so older guides pointing to Callaghan are out of date. You must fund 60% of costs yourself. The R&D Experience Grant is closed for 2026/27; the R&D Career Grant (up to NZ$30,000 or NZ$35,000) is open until funds run out.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

## Philanthropy and AI-focused funders
Foundations and AI labs that fund people, research and model credits; most suit AI safety, research or social impact work rather than ordinary business tools.

### [Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy): Navigating Transformative AI](https://coefficientgiving.org/funds/navigating-transformative-ai/)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Varies
- **Who it's for:** Individuals and organizations working on the risks and governance of advanced AI, including people at any career stage moving into the field.
- **How you start:** Read the open requests for proposals on the fund page (AI Governance, Capacity Building, Career Development and Transition Funding) and apply through the one that matches your work.
- **Why it's here:** A very large AI funder that publishes open calls, including career transition funding for individuals.
- **Caution:** Check first. The focus is reducing risks from advanced AI, not business productivity. A workflow build fits only if it serves AI safety, governance or field building.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [EA Funds: Transformative AI Fund](https://funds.effectivealtruism.org/funds/transformative-ai)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Typically $10,000 to $150,000
- **Who it's for:** Individuals, new organizations and existing organizations with new projects working on the effects of transformative AI.
- **How you start:** Fill in the online application form linked from the fund page; EA Funds says applying takes less than an hour, and the fund is always open.
- **Why it's here:** One of the few funders that gives early-stage grants straight to individuals, with a rolling application.
- **Caution:** Check first. The Long-Term Future Fund has closed and this fund replaced it. Its primary focus is reducing catastrophic risks from advanced AI. Grants rarely exceed $300,000.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [Emergent Ventures (Mercatus Center)](https://www.mercatus.org/emergent-ventures)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Varies
- **Who it's for:** Individuals anywhere in the world, age 13 and up, with ambitious, scalable ideas.
- **How you start:** Submit the short online application; one application per person per project.
- **Why it's here:** Low-overhead fellowships and grants for unusual individuals, with no need to be a company or have a track record.
- **Caution:** Check first. It no longer accepts AI-written applications and says it rejects any that use AI for more than proofreading, so write this one yourself. No feedback or status updates are given.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [Anthropic AI for Science (Claude API credits)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/expanding-support-for-scientists)
*Helps you get: a funder, people who use it*
- **What you can get:** Up to $50,000 in API credits per project
- **Who it's for:** Researchers working on scientific projects; Anthropic's 27 Aug 2026 post says any researcher is eligible to apply.
- **How you start:** Submit the AI for Science application form linked from the post, describing your team and research project.
- **Why it's here:** Free model credits let a research build run real trials with users before anyone has to approve a budget.
- **Caution:** Check first. The older help article still says up to $20,000 over six months for academic and nonprofit researchers, reviewed on the first Monday of each month; go by the newer post. Credits work on the API only, not the Claude app. Anthropic's Economic Futures research grants are not accepting applications.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [OpenAI Researcher Access Program](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10139500-researcher-access-program-faq)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Up to $1,000 in API credits, valid 12 months
- **Who it's for:** Researchers affiliated with an academic institution or research organization, and nonprofits doing research, based in countries the OpenAI API supports.
- **How you start:** Apply any time through the program form (hosted on SurveyMonkey Apply); applications are reviewed in March, June, September and December.
- **Why it's here:** Small but easy credits for studying responsible use, risks and social impact of AI.
- **Caution:** Check first. Research only, on responsible deployment, risk or societal impact. The next review is December 2026, with credits about 4 to 6 weeks after that.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [Google.org Accelerator: Generative AI](https://impactchallenge.withgoogle.com/genaiaccelerator/)
*Helps you get: a funder, people who use it*
- **What you can get:** 2025 round: a share of $30 million plus pro bono help and Cloud credits
- **Who it's for:** Organizations using generative AI for social impact; press coverage of the 2025 call lists nonprofits, civic groups, academic institutions and social enterprises.
- **How you start:** Read the 2025 recipients and focus areas to see what gets funded, then watch Google.org for the next open call.
- **Why it's here:** One of the largest AI-for-good programs, and it pairs grants with six months of help from Google staff.
- **Caution:** Check first. Closed: 2025 recipients have been selected and no 2026 call was found. Applicant types come from press coverage, not the official page.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [Candid search (Foundation Directory)](https://candid.org/candid-search/find-nonprofit-funding/)
*Helps you get: a funder*
- **What you can get:** Paid: from $219 a month; free at Candid partner locations
- **Who it's for:** Nonprofits and fundraisers researching foundations and their past grants, mainly in the US; also useful for consultants who serve nonprofits.
- **How you start:** Check Candid near you for a free partner location first, then search funders by subject and geography and read what they funded before you write.
- **Why it's here:** It shows who a foundation has actually funded and for how much, the quickest way to find funders that match your work.
- **Caution:** Check first. Paid tool. Most foundations fund registered nonprofits, not for-profit businesses, so it is most useful if you are, or work with, a nonprofit.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

## Startup credits and programs
Cloud and model credits cut the cost of a pilot, while accelerators trade cash for equity and suit people building a company, not a side service.

### [AWS Activate](https://aws.amazon.com/startups/credits)
*Helps you get: a funder, people who use it*
- **What you can get:** Founders tier: $1,000 to start (some get up to $5,000); Portfolio: up to $200,000
- **Who it's for:** Startups that are pre-Series B, founded in the last 10 years, with a paid-tier AWS account. Self-funded founders apply directly.
- **How you start:** Sign in with an AWS Builder ID, choose Activate Founders if you are self-funded, add product and company details, link your AWS account and submit. AWS says you will hear back in 5 to 10 business days.
- **Why it's here:** The easiest credits to get without investors, and enough to host a small pilot.
- **Caution:** Check first. The Portfolio tier needs an Organization ID from an Activate Provider (an accelerator, angel group or VC). Credits only work on AWS.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [Google for Startups Cloud Program](https://cloud.google.com/startup)
*Helps you get: a funder, people who use it*
- **What you can get:** Start tier $2,000; funded tiers $200,000 to $350,000 over two years
- **Who it's for:** Startups with a working product and a live company website. Start tier: founded in the past 2 years and planning to raise venture funding. Funded tiers: Pre-Seed to Series A.
- **How you start:** Apply from the program page using a business email that matches your website domain, with your Google Cloud billing account ID ready.
- **Why it's here:** Strong if you build on Gemini or Google Cloud, because the credits also cover Google's models.
- **Caution:** Check first. Google does not accept consultancies, agencies, dev shops, nonprofits or educational institutions, and it screens out websites with a lot of AI-generated content. Many learners in this cohort will not qualify.
- **Link checked:** 23 Oct 2026

### [Microsoft for Startups](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/startups)
*Helps you get: a funder, people who use it*

## Help and search tools
Free advisers who help you shape the ask, and grant databases, mostly paid, that save search time.

### [America's SBDC (Small Business Development Centers)](https://americassbdc.org/find-your-sbdc/)
*Helps you get: a customer, a funder, your own business*
- **What you can get:** Free consulting; low-cost training
- **Who it's for:** Anyone starting or running a small business in the US, through a network of nearly 1,000 centers. Free.
- **How you start:** Find your local SBDC by state or zip code and book a consult; say up front whether you want help with SBIR, government contracts or a pitch.
- **Why it's here:** Free one-to-one advice on your pitch, pricing and funding plan from an SBA resource partner.
- **Caution:** Check first. US only. Adviser expertise varies by center, so ask for someone with technology or funding experience.

### [SCORE](https://www.score.org/)
*Helps you get: a customer, a funder, your own business*

## How to search for more

- Set up saved searches and email alerts once (SAM.gov, Grants.gov, your state portal, UKRI, Find a Grant, BidNet Direct) and let new calls come to you.
- Register early and for free: SAM.gov, Grants.gov and state vendor portals all come before any application. Never pay anyone to register.
- Partner with a university for STTR, I-Corps, BRAID and many UK and EU calls: the researcher brings eligibility, you bring the real problem and the users.
- AI search misses many small and local funds, so check portals directly and ask a person: your SBDC, APEX Accelerator or national agency (for example Vinnova in Sweden, RVO in the Netherlands, Innovation Fund Denmark, FINEP in Brazil, iNNpulsa in Colombia).
- Read the eligibility lines first. Many programs exclude consultancies, sole traders or non-national owners, and some, like Emergent Ventures, reject AI-written applications.
- Talk to program staff before a call opens. IES invites idea calls before each yearly solicitation and NIH encourages contacting program officials, which can save weeks of writing.
- Start with the smallest real yes: a paid pilot, a voucher or a credit grant becomes evidence for the bigger ask later.
- Check status on the official page every time. Several programs here changed in 2025 and 2026 (the SBIR lapse, Innovate UK Smart grants, Callaghan Innovation, the Long-Term Future Fund).

Checked 23 Oct 2026. Amounts and deadlines change, and some pages may have moved since. Always confirm on the official page before you apply. Real government grants never charge a fee to apply. This list is a starting point, not advice about any particular application.
