# Finding money and opportunities

The aim is a short list of real calls the founder could start this month, each
saved in `founder/opportunities/`.

## Start from a list you trust

If the founder has a funding list from a cohort, an accelerator or a local
business centre, read it first. Treat it as a starting point: amounts and
deadlines change, so confirm each one on the official page before you write it
down.

## Work out what fits before searching

Read `founder/me.md`, `founder/idea.md` and `founder/evidence/claims.md`, then
confirm with the founder:

- Country, and state or region if it matters.
- What they are: a person, a small company, a nonprofit, an employee, a
  researcher with a university partner.
- Field and stage: an idea, a working pilot, paying users. Be honest: an idea
  with only simulated evidence is "idea" stage.
- How much they need, and for what (time, data, people, credits, equipment).
- Who could partner with them: a college, a client, a company.

## Six places to look

1. **Government research money for small companies.** In the US that is the
   SBIR and STTR programs (NSF's America's Seed Fund starts with a short Project
   Pitch). Other countries have their own: Innovate UK, the EIC Accelerator in
   Europe, IRAP in Canada.
2. **Procurement portals.** The state, county or national tender site.
   Register as a vendor, pick the service codes that match, and matching
   requests for proposals arrive by email.
3. **Foundations and charities.** They fund a mission. Match it, and read what
   they funded last year.
4. **Company programs.** Cloud and AI companies give credits and small grants
   to builders. Industry groups run pilots with their members.
5. **Schools and colleges.** Find the program lead or the innovation centre,
   send them the work, and ask for a call. A partner institution opens grants
   you cannot apply for alone.
6. **Inside your own company.** Innovation budgets, internal grants, a
   department head with money for a pilot.

For a local business (a shop, a café, a service), add: small business loans
and microloans from the national small business agency or a community lender,
local business grants from the city or region, and pre-sales or
founding-member offers to customers, which are funding and evidence at once.

## Searches that work

Combine the field, the place and the kind of money:

- `[field] grant [country] [year]`
- `request for proposals [service] [state or city]`
- `SBIR topic [keyword]` or `call for proposals [field]`
- `[funder name] past awards [field]`, to see what they actually fund
- `small business grant [city]`, `microloan [country]`

## Reading a call

Check in this order, and stop as soon as one fails:

1. **Eligibility.** Who can apply: country, organisation type, size, partners
   required.
2. **What they fund.** Does the question or service fit their words?
3. **The deadline,** as written on the official page.
4. **The format.** Letter of intent, pitch, full proposal, quote.

## Rules

- Never invent an amount, a deadline or an eligibility rule. If it is not
  confirmed on an official page, write "check".
- Search always misses some. New calls appear every week, so the founder should
  set email alerts on the two or three portals that fit best and look
  themselves.
- Some registrations take weeks (in the US, SAM.gov for federal awards). If a
  call needs one, flag it as the first step.
- Real government grants never charge a fee to apply. Be wary of paid "grant
  finders" and anyone promising guaranteed funding.
- Fewer, better applications. One good pitch that fits beats ten that do not.
